How gears work

Gears transfer rotation through interlocking teeth.

Core principle

A pair of gears has a speed ratio determined by its tooth counts. A 20-tooth driving gear turning a 60-tooth driven gear produces one output revolution for every three input revolutions. External gears turn in opposite directions.

Understanding the concept

Ignoring losses, the output torque increases by the same factor that speed falls. Real gears lose energy through friction and deformation.

Practical context

A clock uses several gear pairs to make its hands turn at different rates.
